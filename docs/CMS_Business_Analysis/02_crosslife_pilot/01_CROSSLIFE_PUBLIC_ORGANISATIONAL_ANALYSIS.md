# CrossLife Mission Network — Public Organisational Analysis

## 1. Purpose and evidence boundary

This document records what can be observed from CrossLife's public website and separates it from internal facts that require stakeholder validation. It is not an approved organogram.

Primary public references:
- Main website: https://crosslife.live/
- Leadership: https://crosslife.live/leadership.php
- Ministries: https://crosslife.live/ministries.php
- Contacts: https://crosslife.live/contacts.html
- Gallery: https://crosslife.live/galley.html

**Evidence classification:** Public titles and listed ministries are OBSERVED. Reporting lines, internal departments, approval powers, meeting structures and workflows are OPEN unless separately confirmed by CrossLife.

## 2. Public identity and mission themes

CrossLife Mission Network presents itself as a non-denominational and inter-denominational Christian ministry based in Dar es Salaam, Tanzania. Public themes include discipleship, teaching, spiritual formation, fellowship, worship, equipping believers for service and marketplace influence.

This public identity suggests that the system should not assume CrossLife operates only as a conventional Sunday congregation. The product model should be able to represent a church/ministry with multiple programmes and ministry expressions.

### Analysis cautions

- A public title is not proof of a formal position description.
- A title does not establish a reporting line.
- A responsibility label does not establish approval authority.
- Two finance-related public roles do not prove the internal finance control model.
- One person may hold multiple responsibilities.
- The public list may be incomplete or out of date.

## 6. Pilot boundary

CrossLife currently has no branches. The pilot should model the current main-church operation and should not introduce branch-management screens, branch reporting or branch oversight workflows. A generic organisational-unit model may preserve future flexibility, but branch functionality is not an MVP requirement.

## 7. Stakeholder validation required

1. What is the formal governance body, if any?
2. Who has authority to appoint, suspend or remove leaders?
3. What is the approved organogram?
4. Which public functions are offices, departments, ministries, teams or programmes?
5. Who reports to whom?
6. Which decisions require approval, and by whom?
7. Are leadership appointments fixed-term, indefinite or event-based?
8. Which units manage their own members, events and communications?
9. What membership categories and eligibility rules are used?
10. Which information may members see about other members?
